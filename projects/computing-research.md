# Computing without binary

Tests, in simulation, of whether a computer could work on something other than binary. Mostly negative, and written down as negative.

**Status:** research, in simulation. No chip has been made. **Reviewed:** 17 Nov 2026.

## What it is

Every computer I use works in binary. I wanted to know whether there is a better base to compute on, so I tested ideas for computing without binary in simulation on my PC. Nothing here was measured on real hardware.

## Why it exists

This is the other half of what I want to rebuild. My learning research asks how a model can learn more from less. This asks the same question of the machine underneath. In my own words:

"I want this to replace binary."

And the hardware aim, which is an aim and nothing more: "I want to design a, a chip that is extremely cheap to produce and ... doesn't use binary."

Nothing on this page shows that such a chip is possible. It is where I want to end up.

## What I built

- Simulations of several ways of computing without binary.
- A written record of every result, the negative ones included.
- A review on 17 Nov 2026, as part of Project Alpha, that re-opened my own chip numbers to see whether they still held.

## Results

The results so far are mostly negative, and I wrote them down that way:

- A ternary cell lost to binary on transistors per bit.
- Stochastic computing was closed as a negative.
- A bit-slicing idea died to both of its controls.
- The 17 Nov review re-opened 21 of my own chip numbers and found 1 bad. I fixed it.

## Where it falls short

My own chip files say it in capitals: "WE DID NOT BEAT BINARY".

The project's notes say it plainly too: "No watt, no wire, no chip is measured here." Everything on this page is simulation.
